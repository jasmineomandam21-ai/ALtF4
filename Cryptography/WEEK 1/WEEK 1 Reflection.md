**OVERALL REFLECTION FOR WEEK 1** *(Learnings and Challenges)***:**  
During the first week of studying cryptography, I was able to move beyond simply reading about concepts and begin applying them through practical activities. One of the most valuable things I learned was how theoretical ideas in cryptography actually work when implemented or analyzed in real situations. At first, concepts like encryption, decryption, and ciphertext seemed straightforward in theory, but working through exercises such as encrypting messages and solving simple cryptographic challenges helped me understand how these processes function step by step. Seeing how readable text could be transformed into seemingly random characters made the purpose of cryptography much more tangible.

A significant learning experience came from solving classical cipher challenges, particularly those involving the Caesar cipher and substitution ciphers. While these ciphers initially appeared to obscure messages effectively, attempting to break them showed how vulnerable they are to systematic analysis. By trying different shift values and observing the results, I was able to recover the plaintext and recognize how small key spaces make brute-force attacks extremely effective. This process reinforced the idea that security does not only depend on encrypting data but also on the strength of the encryption method itself.

Another interesting part of the week was applying frequency analysis to substitution ciphers. At first glance, the ciphertext appeared completely random, which made it slightly challenging to know where to begin. However, once I started examining letter frequencies and comparing them to common patterns in the English language, I gradually saw how hidden structures could be revealed. Identifying common letters and short words helped me reconstruct parts of the message step by step. This activity helped me realize that cryptanalysis often requires patience, observation, and logical reasoning rather than just technical tools.

Although the tasks this week were not particularly difficult, they still required careful thinking and attention to patterns. One minor challenge was learning how to approach encrypted text systematically rather than randomly guessing solutions. Once I began applying structured methods—such as testing possible shifts, analyzing letter frequencies, and identifying familiar word patterns—the solutions became clearer. This experience showed me that cryptanalysis is not just about breaking encryption but about understanding the weaknesses within an algorithm.

Overall, this week helped me appreciate the historical importance of classical cryptographic methods and their role in shaping modern cybersecurity practices. Even though techniques like the Caesar cipher and simple substitution ciphers are no longer secure today, studying and breaking them provided valuable insight into how encryption works and why stronger algorithms are necessary. The activities also helped me build confidence in approaching cryptographic problems and reinforced the importance of analytical thinking when dealing with security-related challenges.
